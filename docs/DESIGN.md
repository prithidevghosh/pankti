# Pankti visual direction

Pankti is the product name. The visual reference is the printed counter ledger and the stainless steel of an Indian lunch service. Use an original, cuisine-neutral monotype on sign-in and as a small mobile crop. Keep the sales ledger quiet and ruler-aligned.

| Color | Usage |

| #702D40 | Deep lac red for actions and the sales masthead |
| #312821 | Warm carbon ink for text |
| #F7F0E3 | Paper ground |
| #D7CAB8 | Ledger rules |

These screen colours interpret a historical pigment palette. The restrained navigation border takes its cue from the narrow red border of an Aurangabad Ragamala folio. The full-colour prints retain their own palettes. [Met collection](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/662718) · [Pigment analysis of the Dispersed Bhagavata Purana](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02491-x)

Set the Pankti wordmark in Instrument Serif. Use Bricolage Grotesque for headings and system sans for functional text and numbers. Open with “Say the order. Get the bill.” and rotate the word order across Indian scripts on the website. Left-align descriptions and right-align monetary columns. Keep the main mic control centered on mobile, with order and check total stacked underneath.

Design review before implementation. A generic dashboard would center a greeting over interchangeable stat cards. Use a single report canvas with typographic hierarchy, real units and a tabular bill history instead. Use an abstract print made from the visual language of steel reflections, receipt paper and sound. Use an original, quiet split-screen composition on sign-in, flat color, clear focus states and no fabricated business figures.

## Artwork

Counter rhythm is a generated monotype-style illustration. It appears on the web sign-in and the mobile sign-in and onboarding screens.

Prompt used with built-in ImageGen:

> Create a single original vertical fine-art print, museum acquisition quality, for Pankti, an operations app for independent restaurants across India. An abstract still life about the rhythm of a service counter: a few intersecting arcs drawn from reflections of stainless-steel vessels and one slender strip of receipt paper rendered as a pure geometric form, with a repeated line suggesting sound. Medium is an ink monotype on heavy cotton rag with visible press textures, deep bottle green, graphite and restrained muted turmeric pigment, bold off-center composition, subtly imperfect ink registration. Use large and small shapes with tension and clear breathing room, as a work in an exhibition of contemporary Indian printmaking, with no imitation of any specific artist. This is a fine-art object, flatly digitized straight on, not a photograph, not a drawing of any recognizable dish or location, no text, no symbols, no logos, no QR code, no outer mockup or picture frame. Composition remains legible in a shorter horizontal center crop.

## Website account screens

Treat visual design as a product requirement. Compose the form and Counter rhythm as two facing pages. Match the paper tone, use Instrument Serif for the large invitation, and let field labels and values share a ruled baseline. Reserve ochre for the submit arrow and microphone. Keep the account switch visible before the fields, give every field a persistent label, and check both forms at a narrow phone width and a short laptop height. Give recording, errors and keyboard focus the same attention as the resting state.

The direct action language in [Cooper Hewitt’s membership page](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/membership/) and the restrained type and rules in its [design journal](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/16_S_DesignJournal_051016_Final-small.pdf) informed this direction.

### Phone layout review

At 390 × 844, signup left 309 px below Create account while the artwork occupied just 59 px. At 430 × 932, the gap grew to 393 px. In 568 × 320 landscape, the sign-in button fell below the screen. Our first review only checked whether signup overflowed.

Review the composition of both screens in portrait and landscape. Keep the fields together and the primary action a comfortable thumb's reach from the bottom. Give spare portrait height to the print. Let the document scroll naturally for larger text, feedback or the on-screen keyboard. Check where the button actually lands and inspect screenshots alongside the overflow measurements.

### Closing the Counter

The overview has its own print. Stacked order slips suggest what the owner sees at closing time, the accumulation of one service. The torn edges and ink transfer connect it to the sign-in monotype. A vermilion impression gives it a distinct focal point that survives the mobile crop. The four operational views use a compact double-ruled header to leave room for work. Give each future print a specific subject and place in the product.

Built-in ImageGen prompt:

> A new abstract fine-art print for an independent Indian restaurant owner's dashboard, titled Closing the Counter (do not print words). Landscape 3:2, single full-bleed digitised handmade collagraph on warm unbleached paper. Subject: the day's handwritten order slips gathered into a stack at closing time. Compose a tight staggered fan of torn rectangular chits and flat carbon-paper impressions pressing diagonally from lower left toward upper right, a few broken horizontal lines like pressure marks transferring between sheets, a single intense vermilion rectangular ink offset amid deep indigo-black, smoky bottle green and buff. Make the silhouette instantly legible at thumbnail size. Physical dry-ink skips, relief edges, uneven embossing in small areas, ample open paper, restrained, editorial, museum print-room quality. Keep the most distinctive overlapping junction in the center so it also reads in a narrow mobile crop. No text, numbers, lettering, food imagery, utensils, circles, arcs, gradients, wall, frame or shadows. Flat print, no mockup.

## Working surfaces

Keep the overview as a daily statement. Put paid sales with the date and the closing-time print. At the counter, place the recording control beside a running paper ticket, with the payable total beside the issue action. For vendors, bind the balance list to a print of market scales and keep the running journal below it. Treat Menu as a book, with a cobalt scanner cover and a cream price list. Set the actual payment code on a free-standing white merchant placard. On phones bring the controls and document into one reading column and keep the five destinations within reach.

Use plum ink #38252f, paper #f7f0e3, ticket white #fffcf6, cobalt #182e72 and claret #643d48. Instrument Serif carries titles and totals. System sans carries editable details. Align names left and amounts right. Keep the quiet zone around each payment code clear. Use the two new built-in ImageGen prints once each.

## Android counter

Design for one-handed service. On signup, keep the account switch at the top, the fields editable after dictation and the form scrollable with the keyboard open. Give the menu its blue folded-paper cover and the order its white running ticket. Place recording and confirmation above the bottom navigation so long menus and orders can scroll independently. Use text and an icon for each recording state. Display the final receipt number, payment state and rupee total separately from the actions that record payment. A cashier must be able to take the next order with one tap after settlement.

Orders and Bank now each have a dedicated print on web and Android. Keep the rail above the recording surface and the stamp outside the QR quiet zone. Package smaller JPEG copies for the Android app.

### Vendor corrections

Keep each posted entry in the list throughout editing. Show the correction sheet alongside the journal on wide screens and below a scrollable list on phones. Keep its original supplier and date visible while editing the name. Use the vendor ledger’s plum ink for the sheet heading. Allow Bengali and other scripts to wrap in names and notes. Mark an unpriced order as “Price pending”. Check the neighboring row, reduced motion, keyboard focus on cancel and a full preview-to-save cycle at phone width.

### The handoff

Signup introduces a human gesture through two ink silhouettes passing a receipt. The desktop composition has open paper above and below. Android uses a horizontal detail centred on the slip.

Built-in ImageGen brief: an abstract collagraph of cropped bottle-green and ochre forearms exchanging a narrow ivory receipt. Use a carved notch for each grip, three broken graphite rules on the slip, a low charcoal counter edge and one brick-red offset.
